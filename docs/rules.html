Rules

Proposed by Paul Spetrini for the 2026 season.

1. Keepers

  • 2 max keepers.
  • A player can be kept twice (3-year total lifespan).
  • Cost rises 1 round from the original draft pick each year.
  • Undrafted player: costs a 7th, then a 6th.
  • Dropping and re-adding a drafted player does not make him "undrafted" — his drafted-round cost follows him all season. The 7th-round cost is only for players never drafted this year.
  • Trading a keeper does not reset his keeper clock.
  • Eligibility: drafted round 5 or later (or undrafted).
  • If you don't hold the pick a keeper costs, either acquire that round's pick in a pre-draft trade (pick trades are uncapped) or pay with the nearest higher pick you own — your 3rd for a 4th-round cost. Never a later/cheaper pick.
  • Each keeper burns its own pick. Two keepers costing the same round require two picks in that round (two 7ths = two undrafted keeps). With only one, the second keeper escalates to your nearest higher pick — a 7th and a 6th.

2. Trade Lockups

  • Players may be traded through the week before playoffs.
  • Round 1–2 future picks cannot be traded or acquired.
  • Weekly deadline: player trades are due by midnight the night before the earliest game involving any traded player. Thursday game = submitted by Wednesday midnight.
  • Pick-only trades have no deadline.

2A. Veto Process

  • 4 veto votes from managers not in the trade sends it to review — same threshold for any trade size.
  • On review, the non-vetoing managers confer by phone, hear the perspectives, and make the final call.
  • Please do not abuse the veto.

2B. The Right to Tank

  • Keeper league, big buy-in — trading away players within the rules is your right.
  • A clearly lopsided or collusive trade can still be rejected via the veto.
  • Teams must field active players (regular season only), barring bye-week shortages.
  • Violations: the commissioner picks two managers not in the matchup to form a 3-person committee that resolves the issue.

3. Waiver

  • FAB budget system: $100 per manager per season, $1 minimum bid.
  • Claim ties are broken by the league's rolling waiver priority list.

4. Reset (2025 only)

  • One-time format for the 2025 soft reset — does not apply going forward.
  • Fixed-order draft rounds 1–4 by last season's ranking, snake from round 5 — a compromise between a full reset and none.
  • 2026 onward uses #5 below.

5. Draft Order (2026 onward)

  • Rounds 1–2: fixed reverse-standings order — worst team picks first, champion last, in both rounds (no snake-back).
  • Round 3 onward: normal snake.
  • Example (8 teams): champion picks 1.08, 2.08, 3.01; last place picks 1.01, 2.01, 3.08.
  • Why: two straight reverse rounds give the worst team a real leg-up (1.01 + 2.01); the snake keeps the rest balanced; contenders get the 2.08 → 3.01 turn.
  • A keeper forfeits that team's pick in its cost round; teams skip rounds their keepers occupy.
  • Optional anti-tank guardrail (TBD by vote): weighted lottery among non-playoff teams for the top of the order.

6. Player Auto-Subs

We're all busy people — the league uses Sleeper's built-in Player Auto-Subs (how it works) instead of manual swaps.

  • An inactive starter is auto-replaced by his designated bench sub.
  • Assign subs in the mobile app; no injury tag required.
  • The sub must fit the starter's slot; each sub covers one starter.
  • Locks once either player's game starts; change subs any time before.
  • League setting: 2 auto-subs per week.

7. Payouts

$300 buy-in per manager, due before the draft — $2,400 pot.

  • $20 per win × 14 regular-season weeks ($1,120)
  • $380 for most points scored
  • $900 for the league champion
  • Ties in any payout stat split evenly among tied managers.

Weekly wins spread the wealth — and the champ or points leader likely wins 8–10 games anyway, adding $160–$200 on top.

8. Pre-Week-1 Housekeeping

Still to finish before week 1 (nothing malicious — just not done yet):

  • Scoring: minor tweaks possible to match the Sleeper settings exactly.
  • Waiver timing: when waivers run will be voted on by the league.

9. Schedule & Playoffs

  • Regular-season schedule and playoff format will be determined after the draft.

10. Commissioner Powers

  • New rules or emergency commissioner actions require a 50% majority (4 of 8), commissioner's vote included.
  • Actions that directly impact the commissioner require 75% (6 of 8).

⚠ These are AI-generated proposals, not official rules. Generated from analysis of the 2025 season (final rosters, draft grades, trades, tanking patterns). Nothing here is in effect — they're conversation starters for a league vote.

Ranked by impact. Each has the reasoning and the main trade-off.

1. Weekly median game

Each week, also play the league median score for a second W/L.

  • Why: cuts schedule luck. This year the best-drafted roster (Devin, A+) finished 4th while below-par drafters rode favorable matchups to the podium. A median game rewards good teams regardless of who they drew, and keeps eliminated teams sweating every week.
  • Trade-off: doubles the number of "games," so records look different (more wins/losses).

2. Draft lottery for the fixed Rounds 1–4

Keep worst-teams-pick-first, but make it a weighted lottery (NBA-style) instead of guaranteed order.

  • Why: with reverse-order fixed picks that can't be traded, tanking is directly rewarded — exactly what happened in 2025 (a team sold vets, finished 7th, banked premium picks). A lottery keeps strugglers likely to pick early without guaranteeing the top pick for losing on purpose.
  • Trade-off: adds a lottery step; a genuinely bad team could slip a couple spots.

3. FAB allowed in trades (+ open R3+ picks, keep the R1–2 ban)

Let managers include FAB $ and round 3+ future picks in trades. Keep the ban on trading R1–2 picks.

  • Why: more trade currency = more deals = more fun. Keeping the R1–2 ban stops a tanker from flipping the reverse-order pick reward for win-now vets and double-dipping.
  • Trade-off: FAB accounting to track; commissioners verify.

4. Trim the bench (5 → 4)

Drop one bench slot.

  • Why: deep benches let strong teams hoard talent (Devin's A+ roster stranded points on the bench; lots of drafted players churned unused). A slightly shallower bench forces tougher cuts → more talent on waivers → more parity and more FAB bidding action.
  • Trade-off: a bit more bye-week/injury juggling; one fewer stash spot.

5. Steeper undrafted-keeper cost (R7 → R5)

Raise the keeper cost for waiver/undrafted players.

  • Why: R7 is too cheap — this year that made Matthew Stafford (335 pts) and Trevor Lawrence (316 pts) keepable for a 7th. A waiver hit shouldn't cost less than a solid mid-round pick.
  • Trade-off: discourages keeping waiver gems; may reduce in-season waiver excitement slightly.

6. Weekly high-score bonus + toilet-bowl stakes

Give the week's top scorer a small reward (an extra win or +FAB), and add real stakes to the last-place bracket (a punishment tradition, maybe a minor draft perk).

  • Why: gives dead/tanking teams something to play for so nobody checks out.
  • Trade-off: more moving parts to administer.

If you do just three: median game + draft lottery + FAB-in-trades. Cheap to adopt, hits fairness, anti-tank, and deal-making without changing the league's identity.
